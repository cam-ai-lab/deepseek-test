# Part 3 — Testing from the outside

Everything in Part 2 ran inside, or right next to, the application's own JVM. Those tests are fast
and precise, and they share something with the code they test: its classes, its configuration,
its assumptions. Part 3 is about tests that share nothing.

Chapter 12 explains what "black-box" really means and why the lab's current system tests don't
qualify. Chapter 13 introduces behaviour-driven development and Cucumber for engineers. Chapters 14
and 15 build a black-box suite that tests the real Docker image of the service. Chapter 16 goes
looking for the bugs nobody wrote a scenario for.

The listings in this part come from the `blackbox/` module on the lab's `main` branch, which
implements the design in `BLACKBOX-TEST-PLAN.md`. The plan and the code don't match perfectly — no
plan survives contact with a real CI runner — and each chapter points out where they diverged and
why. Chapter 14 ends with the story of the suite's first CI runs, which is worth reading even if you
skip everything else.
